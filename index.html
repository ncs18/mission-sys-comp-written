Module 1

Foundational Principles

The electromagnetic spectrum

The EM spectrum is the full range of electromagnetic radiation, differentiated by wavelength (λ) and frequency (f). The two are inversely proportional through the speed of light.

\[c = f\lambda \qquad \lambda = \frac{c}{f} \qquad T = \frac{1}{f} \qquad E = hf\]

Long wavelength ↔ low frequency. Short wavelength ↔ high frequency.

Relative position on the spectrum

Longest wavelength to shortest:

Radio → Microwave → Infrared → Visible → Ultraviolet → X-ray → Gamma

Convention matters for how bands get named: IR, visible, UV, X-ray and gamma are normally described by wavelength; radio bands are normally described by frequency, which reverses the direction of the scale.

Module 2

Radar Systems

Antenna gain

Gain measures how well an antenna focuses power, expressed relative to an isotropic radiator.

\[G_{\mathrm{dBi}} = 10\log_{10}\!\left(G_{\mathrm{ratio}}\right) \qquad G_{\mathrm{ratio}} = 10^{\,G_{\mathrm{dBi}}/10}\]

Radar cross section (σ)

RCS measures how "big" a target looks to a radar. 0 dBsm is not invisible — it is the RCS of a perfect 1 m² sphere.

\[\sigma_{\mathrm{dBsm}} = 10\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{\sigma}{1\ \mathrm{m^2}}\right)\]

Detection range goes as the fourth root of RCS, because the two-way return falls off as 1/R⁴:

\[R \propto \sigma^{1/4} \qquad \sigma_{\mathrm{new}} = \sigma_{\mathrm{old}}\left(\frac{R_{\mathrm{new}}}{R_{\mathrm{old}}}\right)^{\!4}\]

The equation card carries the same relationship in test form:

\[\frac{R_{50,\mathrm{spec}}}{R_{50,\mathrm{test}}} = \left(\frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{spec}}}{\sigma_{\mathrm{test}}}\right)^{\!1/4}\]

Signal-to-noise ratio

SNR is the echo compared to the background noise. A negative SNR does not guarantee you are safe — modern radars integrate multiple pulses to pull weak signals out of the noise floor. Coherent integration of N pulses buys 10 log(N) dB of processing gain.

RCS reduction

Stealth design hierarchy, in order of effectiveness:

1. Shaping 2. Materials 3. Size

Doppler and clutter

Radars use Doppler shift to separate movers from stationary ground clutter. On a Doppler spectrum display, returns to the left of the main lobe carry a negative frequency shift and come from objects receding from the radar.

\[f_d = -\frac{2\dot{R}}{\lambda} = \frac{2V_R\cos\theta}{\lambda}\]

Card convention: range decreasing → negative Ṙ → positive closure → positive Δf.

SAR and beam spot size

SAR synthesizes a long aperture from the platform's own motion. Synthetic beamwidth is inversely proportional to effective aperture length — smaller beamwidth means better cross-range resolution.

\[\begin{aligned} \beta &\approx \frac{0.44\,\lambda}{L_{\mathrm{eff}}} \quad \text{(radians)}\\[4pt] \rho_{CR(\mathrm{SAR})} &= R\beta = \frac{0.44\,\lambda R}{L_{\mathrm{eff}}} \end{aligned}\]

Real-beam spot size uses the small-angle approximation:

\[\text{beam width} = R \times \theta_{\mathrm{beam}} \qquad (\theta \text{ in radians})\]

Module 3

Electro-Optic & Infrared Systems

Imaging fundamentals

EO/IR systems form images by detecting photons.

  • Focal length (f): the distance from the lens to the sensor.
  • Depth of field: the zone of acceptable sharpness in front of and behind the focused subject.

Resolution: IFOV and pixel size

An imaging sensor is a grid of pixels. IFOV is the angle covered by a single pixel and sets the sensor's resolution.

\[X_d \approx f\tan(\mathrm{IFOV}) \quad\longrightarrow\quad X_d \approx f\cdot\mathrm{IFOV_{rad}} \quad \text{(small angles)}\]
\[\mathrm{IFOV_{rad}} \approx \frac{\text{ground footprint of one pixel}}{\text{altitude}}\]

Full-array field of view, from the card:

\[\mathrm{FOV} = 2\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{W}{2f}\right) \qquad \mathrm{IFOV} = 2\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{X_d}{2f}\right)\]

Card equations that support this module

\[\begin{aligned} \text{Lens maker:}\quad & \frac{1}{d_0} + \frac{1}{d_1} = \frac{1}{f}\\[5pt] \text{Hyperfocal:}\quad & H = \frac{f^2}{Nc} + f, \qquad N = \frac{f}{D}\\[5pt] \text{Small angle:}\quad & 1\ \mathrm{mr} \approx 1\ \mathrm{ft}\ \text{at}\ 1000\ \mathrm{ft}, \qquad 1^\circ \approx 1\ \mathrm{NM}\ \text{at}\ 60\ \mathrm{NM} \end{aligned}\]

Module 4

Navigation & Guidance

GPS/INS integration

A Kalman filter blends the two systems. GPS supplies accurate, periodic updates that correct INS drift; the INS supplies continuous data between GPS updates.

Test implication from the deck: when GPS is available and constantly updating the INS, long-duration tests are not required to characterize system performance. The drift never gets a chance to accumulate.

Degraded environments

  • GPS-denied (jammed): the system must rely solely on the INS.
  • GPS-spoofed: more dangerous than jamming. A resilient system needs internal filters that compare GPS against other sources to identify and reject corrupted data.

Kalman filter terms on the equation card

TermMeaning
xState — the thing you want to estimate
zMeasurement — what is directly observable
QProcess noise — unmodeled error on a single step
RMeasurement noise — error on the average sensor measurement
PCovariance — how much you trust the current estimate
rResidual — how far the estimate is off the measurement
KKalman gain — how much you trust the measurement
HObservation matrix — maps state to measurement space
ΦPropagation matrix — moves state forward one timestep

Superscript − is before a measurement update, + is after. A hat means estimated; no hat means truth, which you never actually know.

Module 5

Electronic Warfare

The three divisions of EW

EW is the battle for control of the electromagnetic spectrum, divided into:

  • Electronic Attack (EA) — using EM energy to attack: jamming, chaff, decoys, directed energy.
  • Electronic Protection (EP) — protecting our own use of the spectrum. Spread spectrum is a form of EP.
  • Electronic Support (ES) — sensing and characterizing the spectrum for situational awareness, targeting, and intelligence.

Module 6

Communications & Datalinks

Polarization

If the receive antenna's polarization is mismatched to the incoming wave, you take a loss. Nominal values from the equation card:

MismatchNominal loss
Vertical ↔ Horizontal25 dB
RHC ↔ LHC (opposite circular)25 dB
Slant ↔ V or H25 dB
Linear ↔ Circular3 dB

One-way vs. two-way propagation

One-way link (datalinks, GPS, jamming) — power travels transmitter to receiver once, so received power ∝ 1/R².

\[P_r = \frac{P_t\,G_t\,G_r\,\lambda^2}{(4\pi R)^2}\]

Two-way link (radar) — power travels out to the target and the echo travels back, so received power ∝ 1/R⁴.

\[P_r = \frac{P_t\,G^2\lambda^2\sigma}{(4\pi)^3 R^4}\]

This is why a jammer has a natural advantage over a radar: its signal only makes the trip once.

The card's Adamy/dB forms are usually faster on a written exam:

\[\begin{aligned} S_R(\mathrm{dBW}) &= P_T(\mathrm{dBW}) + G_T(\mathrm{dBi}) + G_R(\mathrm{dBi}) - L_S(\mathrm{dB}) - L_{\mathrm{other}}(\mathrm{dB})\\[5pt] L_S(\mathrm{dB}) &= 32.4 + 20\log\!\left(\frac{\mathrm{Range}}{\mathrm{km}}\right) + 20\log\!\left(\frac{\mathrm{Freq}}{\mathrm{MHz}}\right) \end{aligned}\]

Latency

Latency is the time delay in a communication signal. Beyond-line-of-sight satellite communications carry significant latency because of the distances involved, which makes them unsuitable for time-critical actions such as landing a remotely piloted aircraft.

Module 7

Munitions

Weapon–platform communication lifecycle

Initialization & status check

  • Built-In Test (BIT): when the weapon is selected, it checks its own subsystems — guidance computer, control fins, seeker head — and reports BIT Pass or BIT Fail. A pilot cannot proceed with employment on a BIT Fail.
  • Alignment status: a GPS/INS weapon must align its inertial system to the platform's. It continuously reports Aligned, Aligning, or Degraded. A successful launch requires full alignment.

The pre-launch handshake

  • Master Arm: the operator arms the aircraft's master arm switch — the top-level safety gate that allows power to flow for the final release sequence.
  • Data transfer: the platform sends final refined targeting information — target coordinates, elevation, impact parameters.

Munition markings

Color bands identify type and status. A weapon can carry multiple bands, each indicating the status of a different component.

ColorMeaning
YellowHigh explosive — live warhead
BrownLive rocket motor or low explosive filler — live propulsion
BlueInert — training component (warhead or motor)

Key Supporting Concepts

System development and evaluation

  • SWaP-C — Size, Weight, Power, and Cost. The fundamental constraints in engineering.
  • Tracking vs. fusion — tracking uses a single sensor over time; fusion combines data from multiple sensors.
  • Machine learning — "deep learning" is machine learning that implies artificial neural networks with multiple hidden layers.

Testing and software development

  • System-of-systems testing — challenging at scale because of cost, logistics, and data management.
  • DT campaign — follows a crawl, walk, run progression.
1. Subsystem lab tests 2. Software in a SIL 3. Hardware-in-the-Loop (HITL) 4. Ground tests 5. Flight tests
  • Software development models — Waterfall is rigid and predictable; Agile is flexible and iterative.
